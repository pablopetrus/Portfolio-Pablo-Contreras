Human work when AI gets hands

The kind of conversation you don't think much of at the time. You get on with your day and, months later, you're still thinking about it. With AI, that's happened to me twice.

The first was at an open discussion at Fundación IA Granada. I spoke to someone from a sustainability company and Jevons' paradox came up. Steam engines started doing the same work with less coal. You'd expect less coal to be burned. But running them became more worthwhile, they reached many more places, and total consumption grew. LEDs make it easy to picture: each light uses less energy, but if the same budget now lights the patio, the façade and the street too, you can end up using as much or more energy and getting far more light. I left that conversation and have kept coming back to it ever since.

I think we'll see something similar with AI. If building a tool costs a fraction of what it used to, a company doesn't have to settle for making the same product more cheaply. It can try ideas that never fitted the budget before. Like steam: the efficient engine didn't sit idle saving coal; it went to work in places it couldn't reach before. That will create new work and wipe out some jobs too. Right now I don't think AI will replace us, though it will change how we work enormously.

The second was with my philosophy teacher. He told me about accelerationism in five or ten minutes. Then I did a tiny assignment on it that took another five. Hardly anything compared with the term I spent on Nietzsche, which I loved, or the one on Descartes, which felt endless. Yet that throwaway chat stuck. I started thinking about how each tool can speed up the creation of the next. AI is already helping us build more AI.

That's why my bet changes when I look a little further ahead. In four or five years, I think it could replace an enormous share of human work, especially once robotics lets it leave the screen and act in the physical world. I may be wrong, but that's how I see it today. I wanted to put it in writing so I could come back to it in a few years.
